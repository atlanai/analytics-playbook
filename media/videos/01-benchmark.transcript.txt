Pilots don't stall on the demo. They stall on the tenth real question. It touches two tables and a house rule... and the answer comes back confident, and wrong. So here's what we learned on our benchmark... what gets the tenth question right?

Take one question... what's our Q3 sales pipeline? The query gets the fiscal quarter right, and it returns $18.4 million. Now add one line... and it's $11.2 million. That's the number Finance plans on. No bug here. That rule just isn't in any table, column, or description.

That rule is one of five decisions hiding in this question. Which stages count? Which rows are always out? Which quarter, and which amount, on which table? And how do the rows get put together? Guess each one right 80% of the time... and you'll get all five right about one time in three. That's just arithmetic. On bare schema, only the tables, we measured 31.8%.

So we put it to the test. Our benchmark has 107 real questions, with sealed answers, graded by code. Swap the model, and bare schema scores anywhere from 29% to 40%. Give it five trusted past queries, and all three models we ran scored above 66%. Give it certified metrics with their SQL... and our default model hit 83.2%. So which matters more? Context added 26 points or more to each model we tested both ways. Switching models moved about 10. In fact, the best model without context lost to the weakest model with it.

Now, each kind of context, measured on its own. Descriptions and documentation reached 39.3%. Usage patterns from the query log... 43.9%. Certified metrics, with their SQL, reached 83.2%.

Where does context matter most? In the joins. On bare schema, single-table questions scored 64.3%. Multi-table questions? Just 11.5%. Add certified metrics, and they climb to 92.9%... and 82.0%. And refusals on join questions dropped from 31... to just 1.

Form matters too. Take the same rules, and write them in plain English... then in SQL. On multi-table questions, English scored 65.6%... while SQL scored 82.0%. On single-table questions, they tied... at 95.2%.

Then we rebuilt five published methods on top of our certified metrics... and ran the layer alone, the same way. Four scored lower, by up to 14 points... at three to five times the cost per right answer. The fifth, intent search, gained 1.9 points. That's inside the noise... at twice the cost. Context moved the score. The method of reasoning didn't.

So what does work? A certified metric. It seals every decision into one statement... that runs. Here's our running example. The sales team asks... how much open pipeline do we have with our top 100 customers? Left to guess, the agent said $1.31 billion. With the metric... $412 million. And the business can read it, and correct it. It carries its owners, and a version.

It's cheaper and faster, too. Against the average of the 12 other setups we ran... it cost less than half as much per right answer. And it answered in about a third of the time... 29 seconds a question, against 85. Only curated past queries were cheaper and faster... and they scored 75.7%.

Then choose the lane by asking one thing. If this answer is wrong, and nobody notices... where does it land? The top-hundred pipeline lands in the board pack. So it's governed... a certified metric, or no answer. Which deals slipped? That one's exploratory. The agent writes its own SQL from a trusted past query, and shows it. 96 deals had slipped. And win rate with those customers was 31%... against 24% elsewhere.

So the tenth question never needed a bigger model. It needed the missing rule. Fix the context before you blame the model. Next up: Why evals come first. Because without a golden set, you can't tell a fix from luck.
