Two graders checked the same question against two trusted sources... and got two different hundred customers. The question? Open pipeline with our top 100 customers. The sources? The dashboard tile, and the board pack. So who was right? The RevOps lead chose the board pack... the one the sales team's CRO had promised. This is Phase 1: building the golden set... the test every later change is scored against.

Start with at least 20 real questions... the ones the business already asks. Where do they come from? The board pack, and leaders' dashboards. Analyst tickets from the last two quarters. Saved analyst queries, too. And the sponsor's own questions. Then ask the owner one thing... what was the last wrong number that reached a meeting?

Every item goes in one format, with six required fields. An ID and the question. Its lane, and its source. The expected answer, plus the SQL. Two more fields are optional, and they pay off in Phase 3. The decisions the question hides... and the decoy, the answer you get if you guess them.

Write the grading rules before any result exists. Rules written after... bend toward the results. Grade the data first... then the SQL. A wrong query can land near the right number by luck. Allow valid alternatives, like a total, or a breakdown that sums to it. And a governed answer only counts if it's right on every run.

Then seal the set away from the builders... and guard against three leaks. First, golden SQL in the context. Purge it, and record each removal by hash. Second, the agent reading its own work. Exclude its service account. Third, sealed values in served text. Scan every prompt... a hit invalidates the run.

Now run the bare agent, with only the tables, once per question. It got 7 of 20. Take the pipeline question. It came back at $1.31 billion... but the sealed answer is $412 million. So what went wrong? Four reasonable guesses. It ranked by deal count, counted every stage, summed contract value... and joined each deal to every subscription term. Each guess is a line of the decoy. And the SQL still ran clean. That's Gate 1. The sponsor sees where the agent starts.

Remember that disagreement? It's now a sealed item, with the owner's decision in its history. Seal the questions where the builders can't see them... and grade them by code. Next, Phase 2: turning the SQL your analysts already wrote into certified metrics.
